# The 30-Day Client Sprint

**What this is:** the execution layer. `GROWTH-PLAN.md` explains *why* the
channels are ranked the way they are. This one tells you what to do on which
day, with every message already written. You should never have to open another
file to run it.

**The only number that matters for 30 days:** *conversations booked.*
Not traffic. Not impressions. Not followers. **Target: 8 calls in 30 days.**
At your prices, 8 calls realistically becomes 1–2 paid engagements.

---

## First: should you run Google Ads?

**No. Not yet.** Here is the reasoning, not just the verdict.

**1. Ads multiply a funnel. They don't create one.**
Your funnel has converted zero times. Anything multiplied by zero is zero.
Paid traffic doesn't discover whether your offer works — it just pays to find
out faster, at the exact moment you can least afford the tuition.

**2. You'd be bidding into the most expensive corner of the internet.**
"Laravel developer," "SaaS development agency," "hire Next.js developer" are
agency keywords. You'd be competing for the same click against firms with
salespeople, a review count, and a budget line for customer acquisition. Expect
to spend four figures before the data means anything. (Estimate — the point
isn't the exact number, it's that the learning is not cheap.)

**3. Search intent is wrong for what you sell.**
Nobody types "hire a senior engineer for a 3-week multi-tenant SaaS slice."
People buy this after they *trust* you — from a referral, a post they've read
for months, or a colleague's recommendation. Search catches demand that already
exists and is already shopping. Your category mostly isn't shopping; it's
asking someone it trusts.

**4. You have an asset that is free and enormous, and you aren't using it.**
**~25,000 LinkedIn followers.** If you had to buy an audience of that size and
relevance, it would cost far more than any ad budget you're considering. It is
sitting there, and you have never once used it to say "I am available and this
is what I sell."

Spending money on ads while that sits unused is like renting a billboard when
you already own the building next door.

### When to revisit ads

Come back to this when **all three** are true:

- [ ] You have **2+ real testimonials** on the site
- [ ] You've booked **3+ calls** from free channels (so you know the offer lands)
- [ ] You know your close rate well enough to say what a call is worth

Then start with **retargeting** (cheap, warm — people who already visited) before
you ever touch cold search. And if you go cold, try **LinkedIn Ads by job title**
before Google — you can target "CTO / VP Engineering / Founder at 11–200 person
companies," which is far closer to who actually hires you.

### The Upwork proof play

You have zero public reviews. That is the single biggest hole on your site — it
is the reason a stranger with a $9,000 budget hesitates, and no amount of
copywriting fixes it.

Upwork is the one place where you can **buy proof with labour instead of money**:

1. Take **2–3 small, fast jobs** — $300–800, the kind you can finish in a
   weekend and do flawlessly.
2. Over-deliver dramatically. Fast replies, clean handover, a short Loom.
3. Ask for the review *inside* Upwork the day you deliver.
4. **Then stop.** Do not build a business there — the rates will anchor you low.

You are not there for the money. You are there for three public 5-star reviews
and three quotable sentences you can put on `iamahmedfarid.com`. Treat it as a
30-day errand with an exit date.

**Exit rule:** the moment you have 3 reviews, stop bidding.

## Week 1 — Proof and permission

The goal this week is not a client. It's removing the two things that block
every other channel: no proof, and nobody knowing you're available.

### Day 1 — Ask for testimonials (30 minutes)

Send to three past clients. Pick the ones where you know the work went well:
**Compass Med, Khebra, Proven, Qoralia, Phonic Maps.**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Hope things are going well at [Company].
>
> I'm putting together the public side of my consulting work and I'd love to
> include [Project] — it's one of the builds I'm most proud of.
>
> Would you be willing to write two or three sentences about working with me?
> Anything honest is useful. If it helps, here are the kinds of things that
> land well:
>
> - what the problem was before we started
> - what changed after
> - what I was like to work with
>
> No pressure at all, and if it's easier I can draft something for you to edit
> or reject.
>
> Thanks either way — genuinely enjoyed building that one.
>
> Ahmed

**If they say "can you draft it"** — say yes immediately. Write it in their
voice, keep it modest and specific, send it, and let them change whatever they
want. Most people will. This is normal and not dishonest: they approve it, and
it's their words once they sign off.

**Do not** invent a testimonial nobody approved. One fake quote, discovered,
destroys everything else on the page.

### Day 2 — The availability post (20 minutes)

One post. To 25,000 people. Most of whom have no idea you take outside work.

> For five years I've been building the systems other teams depend on —
> multi-tenant SaaS, real-time bidding under load, platforms in production
> across the Gulf, Europe and the US.
>
> Most of it has been quiet, behind NDAs and other people's brands.
>
> I'm opening two slots for outside work this quarter.
>
> Three ways I work:
>
> → A written architecture review of your system — findings and a prioritized
>   plan you can hand to any engineer. Five days.
> → One production-ready slice, shipped end to end in three weeks. Backend,
>   web, infra, handover.
> → Reserved senior capacity every month, for teams that need a hand on the
>   hard parts continuously.
>
> If you're carrying a system that's outgrowing its architecture, or a build
> that keeps slipping — that's the conversation I'm best in.
>
> Details and eleven case studies: iamahmedfarid.com
>
> Or just reply here. I read everything.

**Post it Tuesday–Thursday, 9–11am Cairo time.** Reply to every single comment
within the first two hours — that's what decides whether LinkedIn shows it to
the other 24,000.

### Day 3 — Build the warm list (45 minutes)

Open a spreadsheet. Five columns: **Name · Company · How I know them · Sent ·
Replied.**

Add 30 people. Not 10. Thirty.

- Every past client contact
- Every colleague from Recovery Advisers, Yelo, RevealSite, iFuture, Compass Med
- Every founder you've ever built something for
- Anyone from Almentor who's now working somewhere interesting
- University friends now at companies with engineering budgets

You are not selling to these 30. You are telling them something.

### Days 4–7 — Message all 30 (15 minutes a day)

Eight a day. Personalise the first line every time — if you can't, they don't
belong on the list.

**To past clients:**

> Hi [Name] — [specific memory of the project, one line].
>
> Quick note: I'm taking on a couple of outside projects this quarter alongside
> my role. Architecture reviews, three-week production builds, or ongoing
> capacity.
>
> Not pitching you — I know [Company]'s situation. But if anyone in your network
> is stuck on a build or carrying a system that's straining, I'd appreciate you
> pointing them my way.
>
> Either way, good to be in touch.

**To colleagues and peers:**

> Hey [Name] — how's [Company] treating you?
>
> I'm opening a couple of slots for outside engineering work this quarter —
> mostly multi-tenant SaaS, real-time systems, and the messy integration work.
>
> If your team is short-handed on something senior, or you hear of anyone
> looking, I'd love the intro. Happy to return the favour any time.

**The referral ask is the whole point.** People rarely need you themselves.
They almost always know someone. Asking them to *think of someone* is a much
smaller favour than asking them to *hire you*, and it converts better.

## Week 2 — Publish and start outbound

### Posting: 3 posts this week

You already have **24 posts written and unused** — 12 English in
`LINKEDIN-POSTS-30-DAYS.md`, 12 Arabic in `LINKEDIN-POSTS-ARABIC.md`.

Stop writing. Start posting. Mon / Wed / Fri.

**Alternate languages.** The Arabic ones are your real edge — almost nobody
publishes senior architecture content in Arabic, and your audience is heavily
Arabic-speaking. Competition there is close to zero.

### Build the target list (60 minutes)

20 companies. Filter hard:

- 11–200 employees (big enough to have budget, small enough that one person decides)
- Gulf, Egypt, or Europe (your timezone, your references)
- Shipping something that looks like work you've already done — marketplaces,
  booking platforms, multi-tenant B2B, anything with real-time or payments

For each, find **one** person: CTO, VP Engineering, or the founder if there's
no CTO.

### 10 outbound messages (Days 10–14)

> Hi [Name] — I came across [Company] while looking at [specific thing:
> the product, a job post, something they shipped].
>
> I build the kind of system you're running — I did [closest case study, one
> line with a concrete outcome].
>
> Not asking for a meeting. If it's useful, I'll send you a short written read
> on [specific thing you noticed about their product or stack] — no charge, no
> follow-up sequence. If it's not useful, ignore this and I won't chase.
>
> Ahmed — iamahmedfarid.com

**Why this works:** it opens with something free and specific, and explicitly
removes the pressure. The "I won't chase" line raises reply rate, and you must
honour it — one follow-up only.

**Follow-up (7 days later, once, then stop):**

> Hi [Name] — closing the loop on this one. Still happy to send that read if
> it's useful, otherwise I'll leave you be. Either way, good luck with
> [thing they're building].

## Week 4 — Count, then decide

Fill this in honestly:

| | Target | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Warm messages sent | 30 | |
| Replies | 8–12 | |
| Posts published | 9 | |
| Outbound sent | 20 | |
| **Calls booked** | **8** | |
| Proposals sent | 2–3 | |
| Paid engagements | 1–2 | |

**Read the result like this:**

- **Lots of replies, no calls** → your *offer* is wrong, not your reach. Fix
  the offer before doing anything else.
- **Few replies** → your *list* or your *first line* is wrong. Narrow the list,
  make the openers more specific.
- **Calls but no close** → your *pricing or proposal* is the problem. Come back
  to me with what they said and we'll rework it.
- **Nothing at all** → you didn't actually send 50 messages. Be honest about
  this one. It's the most common outcome and it isn't a strategy failure.

**Only now** is the ads question worth reopening — and only if you've hit the
three checkboxes at the top of this document.

## The three rules

**1. Reach is not the bottleneck. Asking is.**
You have 25,000 followers and zero clients. That is not a traffic problem. Not
one of those people has been directly told what you sell and that you're
available.

**2. Proof beats copy.**
Two real testimonials will do more for your conversion rate than every word on
your site combined. Get them, whatever it takes — including small Upwork jobs
you'd otherwise consider beneath you.

**3. Consistency beats intensity.**
Eight messages a day for thirty days beats a heroic weekend and then silence.
This whole sprint is under 30 minutes a day. Do the 30 minutes.
